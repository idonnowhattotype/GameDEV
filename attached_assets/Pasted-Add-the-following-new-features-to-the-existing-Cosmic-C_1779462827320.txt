Add the following new features to the existing Cosmic Cleanup Phaser 3 game. Do not break any existing mechanics. Each section is self-contained — implement them in order.

1. Player shooting
Firing:

The player fires projectiles by pressing Spacebar. Rate of fire: one projectile every 0.35 seconds. Holding spacebar fires continuously at that rate. Tapping fires once.
Projectiles travel in the direction the ship is currently facing (use the ship's current rotation to calculate velocity vector).
Projectile speed: 520px/s.
Projectiles are destroyed on contact with the world bounds. They do not wrap.
Maximum 6 projectiles on screen at once. If at the cap, firing is silenced until one clears.

Projectile visual:

Draw each projectile as a small elongated diamond shape in local space: 4px wide, 14px tall. Fill with a gradient effect — white core (#ffffff) inner ellipse (2px × 6px) inside a cyan outer ellipse (4px × 14px, fill #00ffff, alpha 0.9). Add a short tail: a fading line of 3 dots behind the projectile, each 2px radius, alpha decreasing from 0.5 to 0.1 over 10px distance. This should look like a laser bolt.

Hitting enemies:

On projectile–enemy overlap: destroy the projectile, deal 1 hit to the enemy.
Each enemy has 3 hit points (Level 1–2), 4 hit points (Level 3–4), 5 hit points (Level 5).
On each hit: flash the enemy white for 2 frames using setTint(0xffffff) then clearTint(). Play hit sound.
On enemy destroyed: play a destruction sound (see Audio), show an explosion effect at the enemy's position (see Explosion), add 50 points to score, show +50 pop-up at enemy position using the same float-and-fade tween as debris collection.
Enemy is not removed from the scene on first hit — only when HP reaches 0.
Display no HP bar. The flash feedback is sufficient.

Shooting sound:

Add playShootSound() to AudioManager: a very short sine wave blip, 900Hz for 0.04 seconds, triangle wave, gain 0.15, quick exponential fade. This must be quiet and not compete with other sounds.

Explosion effect:

On enemy destruction: spawn 10 small particles at the enemy's position. Each particle is a filled circle (radius 3–5px, randomised), colour randomly chosen from #ff4400, #ff8800, #ffcc00. Each particle has a random velocity (speed 60–160, random direction). Each particle fades out and shrinks over 0.6 seconds using a Phaser tween, then is destroyed. No physics body needed — just tween the alpha and scale to 0.


2. Enemy respawning

When an enemy is destroyed by the player, start a respawn timer.
Respawn delay per level: Level 1: 12s, Level 2: 10s, Level 3: 8s, Level 4: 6s, Level 5: 4s.
When the timer fires, spawn a new enemy satellite at a random position along one of the four screen edges (outside the visible area by 30px). Apply the level's enemy speed.
Show the existing spawn warning arrow for 1.5 seconds when a respawned enemy enters from an edge.
Cap total active enemies on screen at: Level 1: 3, Level 2: 4, Level 3: 5, Level 4: 6, Level 5: 8. Do not respawn if the cap is already reached.


3. Debris splitting

Classify debris into two sizes at spawn: large (base radius 16–20px, worth 10 points) and small (base radius 9–13px, worth 5 points). Spawn 70% large, 30% small per level.
When a large debris piece is hit by an enemy satellite (enemy–debris overlap), do not destroy the debris. Instead, destroy the original piece and replace it with 2 small debris pieces at the same position, each with a randomised velocity that diverges from the original (add ±60° angle variation to the original velocity direction, keep similar speed). Each small piece is worth 5 points.
Small debris pieces do not split further when hit by enemies — they just get scattered (velocity changed, not destroyed).
The debris counter counts all pieces regardless of size. Collecting any piece (large or small) counts as 1 toward the total needed to complete the level.
Visually: large pieces use the existing irregular polygon drawing at full size. Small pieces use the same drawing but scaled down — use a scale factor of 0.6 on the Graphics dimensions.


4. Off-screen enemy indicators
This is separate from the spawn warning arrows (which show for 1.5s on entry). This system runs continuously throughout gameplay.

Every frame, for each active enemy that is outside the visible canvas area (position.x < 0, > 800, position.y < 50 (HUD height), or > 600): draw a small red arrow on the nearest screen edge pointing inward toward the enemy.
Arrow design: a filled triangle (base 10px, height 14px) in red #ff2200 with a white 1px border. Position it on the screen edge at the y-coordinate (for left/right edges) or x-coordinate (for top/bottom edges) that corresponds to where the enemy is heading toward.
Also show for enemies that are on-screen but partially clipped — if enemy centre is within 40px of any edge, show the indicator on that edge.
These arrows are redrawn every frame on a dedicated Graphics layer that sits above the game world but below the HUD.
Maximum 1 arrow per enemy. If two enemies are on the same edge within 30px of each other, show only the one closest to the edge centre to avoid overlap.


5. Level intro card

At the start of each level (when GameScene creates), before gameplay begins, display a full-screen overlay for 2.2 seconds.
The overlay is a semi-transparent dark rectangle (alpha 0.85) over the already-rendered game world (stars and objects should be faintly visible behind it).
Contents, centred vertically and horizontally:

Level name in large bold cyan text: LEVEL 1 (or current level number), font size 52px.
Below it, in white 22px: Collect X debris — Y seconds — Z enemies using the actual values for that level.
Below that, in grey 16px: SPACE to shoot  •  WASD / Arrows to move.


The overlay fades in over 0.3 seconds, holds for 1.6 seconds, then fades out over 0.3 seconds. Only after the full fade-out does the gameplay timer start and enemies begin moving. The player cannot move or fire during the intro card.
Do not show the intro card when returning from pause.


6. Local high score + leaderboard
Storage:

Use localStorage to persist score data between sessions.
Store a leaderboard of the top 5 scores. Each entry contains: score (integer), level (highest level reached), date (short date string e.g. "22 May").
Key: cosmicCleanup_leaderboard.
On game over or mission complete: read the current leaderboard from localStorage, insert the new score if it qualifies for the top 5 (sort descending by score, keep top 5), write back.

Display on MenuScene:

Below the Start button, show a leaderboard panel.
Panel: semi-transparent dark rectangle, border cyan 1px, width 340px, centred horizontally.
Header: HIGH SCORES in small bold cyan text, centred.
Five rows, each showing rank (#1), score (right-aligned, gold #ffd700), level reached, and date. If fewer than 5 scores exist, fill remaining rows with ---.
The current run's score is highlighted in bright white if it just entered the leaderboard (pass a flag through the registry from GameOverScene).

Display on GameOverScene:

After showing the final score, add a line: NEW HIGH SCORE! in flashing gold if the score entered the top 5, or Your best: X showing the player's personal best from localStorage if it did not.


7. Audio additions
Add these to the existing AudioManager:

playShootSound() — sine wave, triangle oscillator type, 900Hz, 0.04 seconds, gain 0.15, exponential fade to 0. Must not be jarring or loud.
playEnemyDestroySound() — noise burst: white noise buffer 0.3 seconds, bandpass filter at 300Hz Q 2, gain ramps from 0.4 to 0 over 0.3 seconds. Should sound like a small explosion pop.
playIntroSound() — a single low rising tone played when the intro card appears: sine wave from 200Hz to 400Hz over 0.4 seconds, gain 0.3, fade out over 0.2 seconds after the rise. Plays once per level intro.


Implementation notes

Projectiles need a Phaser physics group (this.physics.add.group()) with runChildUpdate: true.
Particle explosions do not need physics — use plain GameObjects with tweens.
The off-screen indicator Graphics layer must be cleared and redrawn every frame in update().
localStorage calls must be wrapped in try/catch in case the browser blocks storage access.
The level intro card must block the gameplay timer start — use a boolean flag this.introComplete = false and only tick the timer when it is true.
All existing systems (health, combo, power-ups, level progression, scene transitions, HUD, audio) must continue to work correctly after these additions.
Zero console errors. All five levels must be completable.
